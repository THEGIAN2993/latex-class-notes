\begin{exercise}
	Prove that a non-trivial finite group is not a free group.
\end{exercise}
	\begin{proof}
		We will prove the converse: let $F$ be a free group on a set $X$. We will show that every nonidentity element in $F$ has infinite order, establishing that $F$ must be an infinite group. Suppose $x_1^{\delta_1}\ldots x_m^{\delta_n}$ is a nonidentity element with order $k > 1$. Then:
		\[
			\underbrace{(x_1^{\delta_1}\ldots x_n^{\delta_n})\ldots(x_1^{\delta_1}\ldots x_n^{\delta_n})}_{k\text{-times}} = 1.
		\] 
		For this to be true, it must be the case that $x_n^{\delta_n}x_1^{\delta_1} = 1$. This implies that the word $x_2^{\delta_2}\ldots x_{n-1}^{\delta_{n-1}}$ also has order $k > 1$. We continue this process inductively, and ultimately show that $x_i^{\delta_i}x_{n-i+1}^{\delta_{n-i+1}} = 1$. Since inverses commute, we can reduce our original word down to the identity $1$ from the inside out. This contradict our claim that $x_1^{\delta_{1}}\ldots x_n^{\delta_n}$ is a nonidentity element.

		
	\end{proof}
