\begin{problem}
Let $(M,d)$ be a complete metric space with no isolated points. Prove that $M$ is uncountable.
\end{problem}
    \begin{proof}
        Suppose towards contradiction $M$ is countable. Then we can write $M = \bigcup_{i = 1}^\infty \{x_i\}$, where $\{x_i\}_{i = 1}^\infty$ is an enumeration of $M$. Observe that:
            \begin{equation*}
            \begin{split}
                \bigcap_{i = 1}^\infty \{x_i\}^c
                & = \left(\bigcup_{i = 1}^\infty \{x_i\} \right)^c \\
                & = X^c \\
                & = \emptyset.
            \end{split}
            \end{equation*}
        Since $M$ has no isolated points, each $\{x_i\}$ is closed and nowhere dense. Hence each $\{x_i\}^c$ is open and dense. But surely from the above string of equalities $\bigcap_{i = 1}^\infty \{x_i\}^c$ is not dense, hence $M$ is not a Baire Space. This contradicts the Baire Category Theorem, as every complete metric space is also a Baire space. It must be the case that $M$ is uncountable.
    \end{proof}
